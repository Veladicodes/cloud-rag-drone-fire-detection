DOWNWIND EVACUATION ZONING (ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT AN OFFICIAL SOP)

Define the evacuation area as an arc extending downwind from the incident, not a
circle. The head of a wind-driven fire threatens the downwind sector first and
fastest; flanking and backing spread is slower.

Sizing the arc:
- Centre the arc on the incident coordinates and orient it along the reported
  wind direction.
- Base radius: about 1.5 km for light wind on level ground.
- Add roughly 1 km of radius for every 20 km/h of sustained wind.
- Extend further where continuous crown fuel, steep upslope terrain, or observed
  spotting lie in the downwind sector.
- Widen the arc angle when wind direction is variable or a frontal shift is
  forecast.

Prioritise notification of occupied structures, campgrounds, and any single-road
communities inside the arc, then routes that would be cut by fire crossing them.
Re-issue the zone whenever wind direction shifts by more than about 30 degrees or
wind speed steps up a category.
