ANCHOR POINT AND FLANKING ATTACK (ILLUSTRATIVE — NOT AN OFFICIAL SOP)

Begin containment from an anchor point: a barrier to fire spread such as a road,
stream, rock outcrop, or previously burned ground, from which a control line can
be built without the risk of fire outflanking the crew.

Work from the anchor along the flanks toward the head. Do not start line
construction directly at or ahead of the head under active spread conditions.
The containment priority vector points from the anchor, up the cooler flank
(usually the upwind or downslope side), holding the downwind head for last once
the flanks are secured.

Line spacing and width scale with fuel and slope:
- Light grass, low wind: a narrow scratch line may hold.
- Brush or timber litter, moderate wind: widen the line and remove overhanging
  fuel; expect to hold and improve.
- High fuel dryness or wind above roughly 20 km/h: use the widest line resources
  allow, back it with a burnout where safe, and post holding crews.

Keep one flank always tied to good black or a firm barrier. If a flank cannot be
held, fall back to the next barrier rather than leaving an open line.
