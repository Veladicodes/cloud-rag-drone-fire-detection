Evacuation
- Orient the evacuation arc downwind along bearing 90 deg (03_evacuation_zoning.txt).
- Light wind on level ground: base radius about 1.5 km; add little for wind at 8 km/h, so ~1.5-2 km downwind (03_evacuation_zoning.txt).
- Notify occupied structures and single-road communities inside the arc first (03_evacuation_zoning.txt).

Containment
- Anchor on a road, stream, rock outcrop or old burn and work the flanks toward the head (02_anchor_and_flank.txt).
- Moderate fuel dryness at low wind: a narrower line may hold; widen and remove overhanging fuel where fuel is heavier, expect to hold and improve (02_anchor_and_flank.txt).
- Keep one flank tied to a firm barrier at all times (02_anchor_and_flank.txt).

Dispatch / staging
- Stage upwind, on a road that will not be cut, about 1 km upwind of the incident (05_dispatch_staging.txt).
- First engine or crew to the anchor plus a lookout to a vantage; hold additional resources staged until size-up confirms where they hold (05_dispatch_staging.txt).
- Report to dispatch on a fixed schedule: size, percent contained, resources committed vs staged, projected spread, any change to the evacuation arc (05_dispatch_staging.txt).

Crew safety
- Confirm LCES before engaging: lookouts, confirmed comms with a check-call schedule, two timed escape routes, and a safety zone sized to the fuel and flame length (04_lces_safety.txt).
- Re-brief on wind shift over ~30 deg, a step up in wind, spotting, or loss of a lookout or comms (04_lces_safety.txt).

Size-up note
- Record location, size, fuel type, wind at the incident, and values at risk within the first minutes; this incident does not obviously meet the escalation triggers, so proceed with the initial response and reassess (01_initial_size_up.txt).
